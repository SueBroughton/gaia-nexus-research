## What the exchange establishes

**The concentration argument is Sue's, credited by Brad in the post.** As AI takes over more of the underlying work, capital allocation judgement, certification, governance and fiduciary accountability concentrate upward, and the organisation becomes dependent on the judgement of fewer people. Organisations measure whether the AI is performing, whether output is certifiable and whether the right person has authority to act — but not whether the humans carrying that accountability remain in a position to exercise it.

**Brad's extension is about exposure rather than capability.** What concentrates upward is also exposure to being wrong, and the first thing to degrade under that exposure is not analytical sharpness but the willingness to be the person who says no. Saying no lands on one name; saying yes is spread across everyone who benefited from the speed. The seat ends up held by someone perfectly capable who has quietly stopped using the capability — with no incident, no failed control, and a record that reads as evidence that judgement was applied at every step.

**Two measures are proposed.** Certification load: how many items one person puts their name to in a period, and whether that number is rising. Review time: the gap between a recommendation arriving and its approval, tracked across a population rather than a case, over months. A compressing gap against a flat approval rate means review has become ceremonial without anyone deciding to make it so.

**Brad states the limits of the remedy himself**, which is worth preserving. A cap on what one signature may cover has a real cost: it slows things down, the person imposing it looks like the bottleneck immediately while the thing it prevents never appears at all, and caps get gamed by splitting items to stay under the threshold. It works only where the threshold is set by exposure rather than dollar value, and where somebody looks at what sits just below the line.

## What the discussion adds

**Frederick Redditt — the gap between signal and authority.** A leading indicator may show that review conditions are becoming inadmissible, but it does not establish who may reduce the load, suspend reliance, reset a limit or permit continuation. He proposes a governed transition: condition signal, evidentiary qualification, admissibility, valid authority, execution constraint, consequence record. His closing formulation is that the question is not only whether the signer remains capable but whether the conditions supporting the signature still have standing when institutional reliance is about to form. This is stated within his independently authored Millings Method.

**Brad Wolfe — the companion series, and the routing problem.** Each indicator has a benign and a malignant reading that look identical from inside the review record: contestation declines because nobody has capacity to object, or because the other side learned what gets pushed back and stopped sending it. The comfortable reading is always available and it gets taken, because it describes a team doing well. His answer is a companion series from the other side of the gate measuring not what the reviewer did but what arrived — volume, mix, how many items are marginal. Review time falling while the population gets harder is saturation; falling while it gets cleaner is progress.

Then the asymmetry: a leading indicator works only if somebody is obliged to act on it, and these indicators indict the person who sees them first. A reviewer whose own numbers say review can no longer be sustained is being asked to volunteer that. His remedy is to route them to whoever set the load rather than whoever carries it, and to make crossing the line reset the cap rather than open a conversation. **An indicator that arrives at the person it describes is a diary.**

**Mo Johnson** — that the conditions can fail long before the person does.

The reply drew the requirements apart into three: detecting deterioration, distinguishing saturation from genuine improvement, and ensuring a governed pathway from that evidence to someone with legitimate authority to intervene. And it accepted Brad's routing point directly — if the signal terminates with the person whose conditions it describes, the result is measurement without effective governance, so the transition from signal to authorised consequence matters as much as the quality of the indicator.
